What is a Pokémon TCG starter deck?

A Pokémon TCG starter deck is a ready-to-play 60-card deck of Pokémon, Trainer and Energy cards that you can open and play straight away, with no deck building. The Pokémon Company sells several kinds, from boxes that teach the game step by step to tournament-ready decks built around one strong Pokémon ex.

Today's line-up has four main parts: Battle Academy for learning with someone, Mega Battle Decks (the newest of the "ex Battle Decks") for your first real deck, League Battle Decks for local tournaments, and Build & Battle Boxes for each new set's prerelease. There is also Pokémon TCG Live, the free digital version.

Which Pokémon starter deck should you start with?

Never played, and learning with someone: get Battle Academy. It has three complete 60-card decks, a two-player game board, two tutorial guides, a rulebook, a large metallic coin, damage counters, three deck boxes and a code card for Pokémon TCG Live. The 2024 edition is built around Armarouge ex, Pikachu ex and Darkrai ex, and was $24.99 at US retail.

Never played, on your own: download Pokémon TCG Live. It's free, it teaches the rules with a tutorial, and the codes inside many products unlock decks in the app.

Know the basics and want your own deck: pick a Mega Battle Deck. Each is a ready-to-play 60-card deck marked Play Level 1, the easiest level on the box. The first pair, Mega Gengar ex and Mega Diancie ex, came out on October 24, 2025. They usually sell for around $15 at US stores.

Want to play at a local tournament: get a League Battle Deck ($29.99). Each one is a 60-card, Play Level 3 deck with 6 damage-counter dice, a coin-flip die, 2 coin condition markers, a deck box, a strategy sheet and a code to play it online. The newest is Mega Greninja ex (November 11, 2026), with Mega Starmie ex and Meowth ex. Mega Lucario ex (May 22, 2026) comes with three Mega Lucario ex and a Secret Box.

Want to try each new set: pick up a Build & Battle Box at a prerelease. Each one has booster packs from the new set, a foil promo card and a pre-built evolution pack, which together make a 40-card deck for the prerelease tournament.

Mega Evolution: the current era

The Pokémon TCG is in its Mega Evolution series, which began in September 2025. Mega Pokémon ex are the strongest cards in it, but they are worth three Prize cards when Knocked Out, instead of two for a regular Pokémon ex. Most current starter decks are built around one of them, so it's worth learning that trade-off early.

How a Pokémon deck works

Every starter deck follows the same deck-building rules, so it's also a template for your first custom deck:

  • Exactly 60 cards, with no more than four copies of any card with the same name, apart from basic Energy.
  • Three kinds of cards: Pokémon, Trainers (Items, Supporters, Stadiums and Pokémon Tools) and Energy.
  • Six Prize cards: you take one each time you Knock Out a Pokémon, two for a Pokémon ex and three for a Mega Pokémon ex. Take them all to win.
  • Other ways to win: your opponent has no Pokémon left in play, or can't draw a card at the start of their turn.

A note on Standard

Standard, the main tournament format, uses regulation marks: the letter at the bottom left of each card. Since April 10, 2026 (March 26 in Pokémon TCG Live), cards marked G are no longer legal, and cards marked H, I and J are. Battle Academy is made for learning and casual games, so its older cards don't need to be Standard-legal. If you plan to play at a store, check the marks or pick a recent League Battle Deck.
