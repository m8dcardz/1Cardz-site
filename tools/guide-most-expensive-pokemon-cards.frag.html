The most expensive Pokémon card ever sold is a Pikachu Illustrator graded PSA 10, which sold for $16,492,000 at Goldin on February 16, 2026. Guinness World Records confirmed it as the most expensive trading card of any kind ever sold at auction. Its seller, Logan Paul, had bought it privately in 2021 for $5,275,000.

2026 has been a record year for high-end Pokémon cards. Below are the ten biggest public sales, the rarest cards behind them, and what actually makes a Pokémon card worth millions.

The cards behind the records

Pikachu Illustrator (1998)

Pikachu Illustrator is the rarest and most valuable Pokémon card. It was never sold in packs: the Japanese magazine CoroCoro Comic gave it to the winners of its Pokémon art contests in 1997 and 1998, 39 copies in all. The card art is by Atsuko Nishida, the original designer of Pikachu, and instead of "Trainer" the card says "Illustrator". Only one copy has ever been graded PSA 10, the $16.49 million card.

"Raincoat" Pikachu and the Art Academy cards (2015)

In 2015, a worldwide contest tied to the Nintendo 3DS game Pokémon Art Academy turned fans' drawings into real cards, and each winner received 100 printed copies of their card. Y. Fujishima's Pikachu in a red raincoat sold for $8.4 million in September 2026, and Daisuke Ochiai's Mudkip sold for $3.72 million and then $4.88 million. These prices jumped very fast: other PSA 10 Raincoat Pikachus sold for under $30,000 in 2025, and Kotaku questioned how the price could leap so far in one year, so treat this market with caution.

Trophy Pikachu (1997–1998)

Trophy Pikachu cards were prizes for the top finishers at the first official Pokémon card tournaments in Japan. The art, by Mitsuhiro Arita, shows Pikachu holding a gold (No. 1 Trainer), silver (No. 2) or bronze (No. 3) trophy. A Gold Trophy Pikachu graded PSA 8 sold for about $3.08 million at Goldin in October 2026, and a PSA 10 Bronze Trophy Pikachu sold for $1,769,000 in May 2026.

Charizard, the most famous English card

Charizard doesn't make the top 10, but it holds the records for regular pack cards. A 1996 Japanese Base Set Charizard with no rarity symbol, graded PSA 10, sold for $1.7 million in March 2026. The English 1999 Base Set 1st Edition Charizard passed $1 million in 2026 too: a PSA 10 sold for about $1.38 million in October 2026. PSA has graded 125 copies at PSA 10, which is rare for a card that came in booster packs.

What makes a Pokémon card expensive?

1. Very few copies. The top cards were prizes, not pack cards: 39 Illustrators, 100 copies of each Art Academy card, and a handful of Trophy Pikachus.

2. The grade. Grading companies like PSA and CGC score a card's condition out of 10. A perfect 10 can be worth several times a 9, as the Pikachu Illustrator shows above.

3. The Pokémon. Pikachu and Charizard sell for far more than other Pokémon. Eight of the top ten sales are Pikachu cards.

4. The story. A famous owner or a famous moment adds value. Logan Paul's Illustrator, which he wore to his WWE debut, more than tripled in price in five years.

The rarest Pokémon cards

"Rarest" and "most expensive" usually overlap, but not always. Some prize cards exist in even smaller numbers than the Illustrator but sell for less because fewer collectors chase them. Modern sets have their own rare cards too, such as Special Illustration Rares, but they come in booster packs and are printed in far larger numbers, so even the best of them sell for a small fraction of the cards on this page.

Quick answers

What is the most expensive Pokémon card? The PSA 10 Pikachu Illustrator, sold for $16,492,000 at Goldin on February 16, 2026.

What is the rarest Pokémon card? Pikachu Illustrator: only 39 were awarded as prizes in 1997–1998, and just one has a PSA 10 grade.

What is the most expensive Charizard card? A PSA 10 1996 Japanese Base Set Charizard with no rarity symbol, sold for $1.7 million in March 2026. The English 1st Edition Charizard record is about $1.38 million (PSA 10, October 2026).

Could my old cards be worth this much? Almost certainly not. The record cards are prizes or perfect-condition first prints. If you have old holographic cards in very good condition, a grading service can tell you their condition, and recent sold listings will tell you what they are worth.
